heading: How do supermassive black holes flicker?
shortHeading: Reading the flicker
question: What can changing light reveal that a still image cannot?
order: 2
introduction: >-
  Active galactic nuclei flicker, but not because Earth’s atmosphere makes their light twinkle. Their brightness changes are intrinsic, arising as matter and energy move through the unresolved regions around a supermassive black hole. The amplitudes, timescales, and delays between different wavelengths can reveal the size and structure of those regions and help constrain black-hole mass and accretion rate. In carefully selected applications, such variability may even help turn quasars into cosmic distance markers, although that use remains under active investigation.

A quasar light curve is a kind of cosmic seismograph. Time delays between colours let us measure otherwise invisible structures in light-days rather than pixels.
